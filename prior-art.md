# Hartree method-aware prior-art audit

Ordinary-context audit, 9 October 2026, started 15:50:38 UTC. This does not reassess the admission rating, certify the developing proof, or propose a generalization. Sources were accessed today. Machine-readable queries, coverage limits, and file hashes are in `search-log.json`.

## Finding

No inspected source states or directly proves the frozen global small-charge quartic Hartree ball theorem. **The central proof mechanism is nevertheless established prior machinery.** Cavallina supplies a general local uniqueness theorem for parameterized nondegenerate critical shapes, together with symmetry inheritance. Georgiev–Prinari–Visciglia previously combine all-minimizer localization, translation fixing, and IFT uniqueness to prove exact radiality under a small repulsive quartic Hartree perturbation. Pacard–Sicbaldi supplies the familiar boundary-flux and spherical spectral framework.

The possible new result is therefore a model-specific application: verify the moving-domain selfconsistent state/physical-flux map in the required Hölder spaces, correctly handle volume and translation, identify the branch with actual minimizing states, and combine local rigidity with the source's global localization. Neither “ordinary IFT gives symmetry” nor “small nonlocal repulsion preserves symmetry” is a new principle. I did not find a paper already performing those Hartree shape verifications. That is a bounded negative finding, not proof of absence.

## Exact target retained

For some q0>0 and every 0<q<q0, the ball is the unique global minimizer, modulo translations and the frozen Sobolev representative convention, among all admissible domains in R³ of volume 4π/3, of

`E_q(Ω)=inf_{u∈H¹_0(Ω), ∫u²=1} [∫|∇u|² +(q/2)∬u(x)²u(y)²/|x−y| dxdy]`.

No smoothness, boundedness, connectedness, or near-ball assumption is imposed on the competing domains. The source's Theorem 1.2 supplies global minimizer existence and near-sphericity; Remark 1.3 asks exact ball uniqueness. The current arXiv record remains v2, 5 February 2025. The author-maintained [CvGMT record](https://cvgmt.sns.it/paper/6581/) was modified 15 July 2026; its [linked PDF](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/6581/MMR-revised_allblack.pdf), pp.2–3, still retains Remarks 1.1 and 1.3. The [published article](https://doi.org/10.1080/03605302.2025.2511919) is CPDE50(8), 1029–1073 (2025), online 3 July 2025. Its coefficient is q/2, matching the freeze.

## Closest general theorems: actual hypotheses

**Cavallina, JDE383 (2024), 1–23**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2023.11.011), preprint [arXiv2109.12559v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12559), 26 September 2021, titled *How to construct parametrized families of free boundaries near nondegenerate solutions*. Theorem I, pp.2–4: a smooth compact-boundary base domain; Banach perturbation and parameter spaces; a Fréchet differentiable shape functional with boundary first-variation density g; a differentiable extension E whose base trace is ξn; differentiability of the pulled-back density into a Banach space Y⊂L²; vanishing base density; and bijective Hessian representative Q:X→Y. Then each small parameter has a unique nearby solution within the chosen chart. The conclusion contains an **if and only if**, so it is not merely branch existence. Remark 1.3 permits arbitrary Banach parameters; Remark 1.1 explicitly accommodates different X,Y. Its two-phase example chooses X=C²,α and Y=C¹,α. These are generic hypotheses, not a Hartree verification. A publisher access succeeded earlier in this tranche but a later ScienceDirect reopening errored; the complete preprint was inspected and saved.

**Cavallina, JGA34:26 (2024)**, [published full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12220-023-01467-8), [arXiv2203.12950v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12950), 2 December 2022. Definitions4.1–4.2 and LemmasI–II, pp.9–12, require a C^{m+2,α} base shape, differentiable shape functional and pulled-back first-variation map `h:C^{m+1,α}×Λ→Y`, and bijective linearization on the appropriate tangent. LemmaII fixes barycentre by the translation-invariant extension `J(ω−Bar(ω),λ)` and supplies local uniqueness of **all** barycentre-fixed critical shapes. TheoremII adds rotational invariance and concludes inherited rotational symmetry. Importantly, this framework does not automatically impose fixed volume: a volume chart or a correctly augmented Lagrangian system remains necessary. Radial symmetry alone is not a global arbitrary-domain classification. Theorem7.1 illustrates torsion with a volume Lagrangian, not Hartree. No Hartree application was found in the inspected text.

The preceding theorems are genuine method overlap. Their differentiability and invertibility hypotheses contain precisely the analytic work the intended proof must establish. Invoking them does not automatically establish that work.

## Other close mechanisms and their exact scope

**Georgiev–Prinari–Visciglia**, [AIHP AN29 (2012), 369–376](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPC_2012__29_3_369_0/), [arXiv1109.3964v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.3964), 19 September 2011. Theorem0.1 concerns fixed L²-mass minimizers on all of R³ of kinetic energy plus repulsive quartic Coulomb energy minus `(1/p)∫|u|^p`, with 2<p<3 and sufficiently small mass. Proposition1.2 localizes every minimizer near the unique unperturbed radial state. Propositions2.1–2.3 fix translations, construct the radial branch, and give uniqueness for the projected critical equation. Section3 identifies every localized minimizer with that branch. This is an especially close conceptual predecessor. It lacks a support-volume constraint and moving boundary; its whole-space H¹ equations do not supply the required domain-to-flux theorem.

**Pacard–Sicbaldi2009**, [primary PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.2438.pdf): Proposition3.2, pp.522–523, establishes smooth dependence of the pulled-back normalized first eigenpair on a C²,α graph. Proposition4.2, p.531, gives the first-order boundary operator C²,α→C¹,α with translation kernel and inverse on its complement. Proposition5.1 uses IFT. This already covers the q=0 boundary architecture; a fresh derivation may be useful for self-containedness but should not be presented as a new spectral principle.

**Domínguez-Vázquez–Enciso–Peralta-Salas**, [arXiv1711.08649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.08649), §2 AssumptionA and Proposition6.1, pp.30–31: local semilinear `Δ_g u+λf(p,u)=0`, radial reference state, invertible state linearization, and nondegeneracy of higher spherical modes. For position-independent nonlinearity on compact or homogeneous manifolds, nearby centered equal-volume solution domains and states are unique under a local isometry; stabilizer symmetries are inherited. The smallness condition includes the pulled-back state's C²,α distance, not just the domain. This is actual uniqueness/symmetry prior art, but f(p,u) is pointwise and does not cover the selfconsistent Coulomb term as stated.

**Allen–Kriventsov–Neumayer**, [arXiv2107.03495v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03495), 20 July 2022: Definition2.11 imposes boundedness, symmetry, state/domain Lipschitz control, and a specified first-variation representation on admissible perturbations h. Theorem2.13 gives regularity/localization for perturbed `λ1+T·torsion` energies. Theorem2.14 assumes a quantitative near-minimizer stability inequality and transfers it globally for that base functional. It does not state that every arbitrary smooth invariant perturbation preserves exact ball optimality. Its uΩ is the linear first eigenfunction; identifying a Hartree energy difference as admissible still requires verification.

**Dambrine–Lamboley**, [primary manuscript](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~jimmy.lamboley/fichiers/Stability22-02-18-Final.pdf), Theorems1.1 and3.2: C³ base boundary, twice differentiable shape functional, Hessian coercivity structure `(CH_s)`, an improved Taylor remainder `(IT_{H^s,X})`, criticality and strict positivity yield quantitative local stability, with volume/translation versions. This is a different general route. Mere C¹ flux regularity is not the same as having already checked its coercivity and improved remainder hypotheses.

**Joseph W. Jerome**, [author PDF](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~jwj/preprints/NFAOFJ/NFAOFJ.pdf), *A Variational and Regularization Framework for Stable Strong Solutions of Nonlinear Boundary-Value Problems*: Remark1.1 fixes a bounded connected C² domain. Definition3.1 and Theorem3.1, pp.12–13, prove continuous Fréchet differentiability of `u↦−Δu+(|x|^{-1}*u²)u` from H² to L²; §§3.2,5 discuss IFT under uniqueness/invertibility for source perturbations. This is prior Hartree state calculus, but not normalized eigenstate/variable-domain/physical-flux calculus. Publication dating was not independently settled beyond the accessible author preprint and 2023 bibliographic discovery; no date inference is needed for the comparison.
